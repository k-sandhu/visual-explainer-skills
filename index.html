Explainers a model built from real data

These lessons, replays and interactive charts were built by Claude using the visual explainer skills, from the saved results of real experiments. Each one is a single HTML file. Tap through any of them; every number traces to the experiment it came from.

The skills first decide what the reader must understand, then pick the form that teaches it best. The gallery is grouped by those forms, with a live example at the top of each group.

Make your own

In Claude Code, add the plugin:

/plugin marketplace add k-sandhu/visual-explainer-skills
/plugin install visual-explainers@visual-explainer-skills

Then give the model your data and ask in plain words: "make a short lesson on why this check can be tricked", "replay these agent traces", "let finance managers change the price and see which option is cheaper". You do not need to name a form. On claude.ai, upload the skill zips from the latest release.

What every example has in common

One message per explainer, and one idea per step. The parts are named before anything moves. Counts carry their denominator, small samples show the range the data allows, and modelled or illustrative numbers are labelled where they appear. Each build was checked at phone and desktop width by the kit's render check, which walks every step and every control.

The examples were built without human edits to the explainers. Some came from the skills' test rounds and use an earlier version of the kit; the newest builds are marked.

Data: experiment-notebooks, one notebook per experiment, plus the paper Organizing Intelligence (Imas, Koh and Legg, 2026, CC BY 4.0) for the two economics lessons. Those two are an independent reading of the paper, not affiliated with its authors.
